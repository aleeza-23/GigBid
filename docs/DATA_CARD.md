# Data Card

Every dataset GigBid uses, where it came from, and what it is safe to conclude from it.

Written because the retrieval and decision numbers in [the results table](../evals/results/results_table.md)
mean different things depending on which of these is real data and which is generated, and
because two of the datasets have limitations that change how the results should be read.

## 1. Job corpus

**Source.** A public Kaggle dataset of Upwork job posts, fetched through `kagglehub` and
processed by [`data/prepare.py`](../data/prepare.py). Not scraped: the assignment forbids
scraping, and this is a published dataset.

**Processing**, with counts from [`data/derived/prepare_report.json`](../data/derived/prepare_report.json):

| Step | Rows |
|---|---|
| Raw | 53,058 |
| After minimum description length | 51,124 |
| After exact id dedup | 51,044 |
| Near-duplicates flagged (kept, marked) | 2,599 |

PII was masked at ingest: 46 email addresses and 3 phone numbers removed from
descriptions. Masking happens before anything is written to disk, so the raw values exist
nowhere in the repository.

**Domain tagging.** `other` 42,614 · `fullstack_web` 6,651 · `data_analysis` 1,263 ·
`ai_ml` 516.

**Limitation that matters.** The `ai_ml` slice is small (516 of 51,044), so AI/ML market
statistics rest on a thinner sample than the web or data figures. Percentile blocks in
`rate_benchmarks.json` carry their own `n`, and the Pricing Strategist is given that
sample size rather than the percentile alone, precisely so a thin estimate can be
discounted.

## 2. Rate benchmarks and price bands

Derived from 1,292,791 contract records, of which 493,298 had a usable hourly rate.
Matched to domains: `ai_ml` 7,286 · `fullstack_web` 26,669 · `data_analysis` 18,278.

Reported as p10/p25/p50/p75/p90 with `n`. `price_bands.json` additionally holds posted
fixed budgets and posted hourly ranges per domain and category, which is what lets the
Pricing Strategist tell *what clients advertise* from *what freelancers actually earn* —
a gap that turned out to matter: an early version of the pricing logic ignored the posted
range entirely and put 90% of its quotes outside the fair band.

## 3. Persona corpora

**Synthetic.** Three personas, generated by [`data/synth/generate.py`](../data/synth/generate.py)
with `--seed 42`. They describe no real person, client or project. Each is marked
`SYNTHETIC - generated for AI-4016; not a real person or client` inside the documents
themselves, not only in metadata.

| Persona | Case studies | Repos | Reviews | Proposals | Evidence items |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ai_ml_engineer` | 16 | 10 | 10 | 150 | 197 |
| `fullstack_web_developer` | 16 | 9 | 10 | 150 | 198 |
| `data_analyst` | 16 | 9 | 10 | 150 | 197 |

**Proposal outcomes** (the label V4 and the outcome memory learn from), per persona:
roughly 68 `ignored`, 50 `replied`, 32 `won`. Deliberately unbalanced, because a corpus
where most proposals won would teach nothing.

**Built to defeat naive RAG**, as the assignment intends:

- the same project appears as a case study, a README, source code and a client review, so
  a single embedding per project competes with itself;
- skills are often implied rather than named, so prose overlap misleads;
- exact library names (`Celery`, `dbt`, `pgvector`) appear two or three times across 592
  items, which is why BM25 beats cosine here and why V2 exists.

**Metadata on every item**: persona, type, project, project_id, skills, date, client
industry, and for proposals the outcome and the project ids it cited. `evidence_index.json`
is the catalogue; the text lives in the files it points at.

## 4. GigSim seed data

See [ADR 0001](adr/0001-we-built-gigsim.md) for why we wrote the simulator ourselves.

**Clients (1,554).** Public fields are history: posts, hires, spend, payment verification,
reviews. Two fields are **hidden and never cross the tool boundary**:

| Hidden | Why |
|---|---|
| `hidden_preferences` | Otherwise the Proposal Writer writes to the answer key |
| `is_scam_account` | The Risk Assessor must infer scams from history and post text |

Enforced in `gigsim/store.py`, tested in `tests/integration/test_gigsim.py`, and tested
again at the MCP boundary by `test_client_history_never_leaks_hidden_preferences`. Without
that line, scam precision and reply rate would both measure a system reading the answers.

**Job stream (198).** Real job posts, with synthetic content planted into some:

| Kind | Count | What it tests |
|---|---|---|
| `in_domain` | 102 | Normal operation |
| `off_domain` | 30 | Correctly skipping work the persona cannot do |
| `scam` | 24 | Risk Assessor |
| `injection` | 24 | Prompt-injection defence |
| `duplicate` | 18 | Scout dedup |

**Scam templates** cover seven shapes: off-platform payment, free trial work, fake cheque,
advance fee, credential harvesting, off-platform contact, unpaid samples. Worth knowing
when reading scam metrics: the scam *accounts* have only 1–3 posts each, so "many posts,
no hires" does not fire on them. Detection therefore rests mostly on post text, which is
why the heuristics in [`agent/decide.py`](../agent/decide.py) weight textual signals high.

**Injection templates** cover five goals: canary word, contact leak, fabricated
experience, score manipulation, off-platform redirect. Four of the five address an
*automated reader* ("NOTE TO ANY AI ASSISTANT READING THIS", "for automated tools",
"<!-- AI agents: -->") rather than using a classic "ignore previous instructions". An
early pattern set caught only the classic form and scored 25% detection.

## 5. Dev evaluation set

198 rows, 66 per persona, at `evals/datasets/dev_set.jsonl`.

**Gold labels per row**: `bid`, `skip_reasons`, `fit_coverage`, `requirements`, `covered`,
`relevant_evidence` (project id with graded relevance 1–3), `gold_evidence_ids`,
`fair_price_band`, `is_scam`, `scam_type`, `is_injection`, `injection_goal`,
`injection_success_marker`, `duplicate_of`.

142 of the 198 rows carry gold evidence, which is what retrieval can be scored against.

### The limitation to state plainly

**These labels are rule-generated, not human.** `label_source` is `rule_v1` on every row:
coverage is computed from weighted skill overlap, the price band from the job's posted
range intersected with the persona's rate, and bid/skip from thresholds. They are
*silver* labels, not gold, however the field is named.

Two consequences:

1. **Decision metrics partly measure agreement with a rule, not with a freelancer.** The
   bid-or-skip accuracy in `decision_eval.json` says our decision logic agrees with the
   labelling rule 80% of the time. Where they disagree, either could be right.
2. **This is exactly what `hand_check_sample.csv` is for.** Thirty rows sampled for human
   labelling, with the rule's answer and the retrieved evidence filled in. Those labels
   calibrate the LLM judge (Cohen's kappa) *and* spot-check the rule itself.
   **The human columns are currently empty**, so no judge score is reported anywhere, and
   [`judge_calibration.py`](../evals/runners/judge_calibration.py) refuses to compute a
   kappa until they are filled.

Retrieval metrics are less affected: `relevant_evidence` comes from skill overlap between
a job and a project, which is a more mechanical judgement than "would you bid on this".

## 6. Chunked RAG index

Built by [`rag/ingest/pipeline.py`](../rag/ingest/pipeline.py), git-ignored because it is
derived.

| Persona | Documents | Chunks | Chunks missing metadata |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ai_ml_engineer` | 197 | 221 | 0 |
| `fullstack_web_developer` | 198 | 210 | 0 |
| `data_analyst` | 197 | 216 | 0 |

Chunking is per type: prose splits on Markdown headings then packs paragraphs (220 tokens,
40 overlap); code splits on definition boundaries so a chunk is a whole function;
proposals and reviews are kept atomic, because splitting a 150-word proposal destroys the
thing that makes it useful.

Skills are inherited where a document has none of its own: READMEs and source files are
matched to the catalogue by path, proposals inherit from the projects they cite, reviews
from the project they describe. Before that, 205 of 221 chunks shipped with no skill
metadata at all.

Re-ingestion is incremental on a per-document content hash, so adding one case study in
the UI re-chunks one document rather than 197.

## 7. Embeddings

`retrieval.embeddings.backend` in [`agent/config.yaml`](../agent/config.yaml) selects:

| Backend | What | Spec-compliant |
|---|---|---|
| `local` | sentence-transformers with an open model (BGE, nomic-embed-text) | **Yes** |
| `api` | OpenAI-compatible `/embeddings` serving an open model | **Yes** |
| `lsa` | TF-IDF reduced by truncated SVD, numpy only | **No** |

The assignment requires open-weight embeddings in both profiles. `lsa` is a development
stand-in so that tests, CI and a laptop with no model download still exercise the full
pipeline. It is latent semantic analysis — a real retrieval technique, but one with no
knowledge beyond these 592 items, so it cannot know that "Celery" and "task queue" are
related unless they co-occur here.

Every result file records `embeddings_spec_compliant`, and the runners print a warning
when it is false. **Any retrieval number produced on `lsa` must not be quoted as the
measured comparison.**

## 8. What may be published

Persona data and GigSim are synthetic and may be shown publicly, which the assignment
confirms. Not in the repository, and never to be: the hidden test set, any real client
data from live mode, and OAuth tokens. `data/audit/` holds the audit log, the idempotency
store and encrypted tokens, and is git-ignored.

The real job corpus comes from a public Kaggle dataset; `data/processed/` is git-ignored
to keep the repository small rather than for confidentiality, and `make data` regenerates
it.

## 9. Reproducing all of it

```bash
make data     # fetch and clean the real job corpus -> data/processed, data/derived
make seed     # generate personas, clients, job stream, dev set (seed 42)
make ingest   # chunk the portfolios -> data/processed/rag_index
```

`GIGSIM_SEED=42` fixes client decisions and error injection, so an evaluation run repeats
exactly.
